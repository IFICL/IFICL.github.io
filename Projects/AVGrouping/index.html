Abstract

In this paper, we address the problem of learning to group image patches into sound sources, using supervision provided only from raw audio-visual data. For this, we propose a method based on contrastive random walks. We train a random walker to move along a graph whose nodes correspond to image patches and audio examples. The model is constrained to learn a cycle-consistency walk: associations that it learns should allow it to return to an image patch after transitioning across modalities to audio. The probability of the walker visiting a pair of examples on this walk then indicates the degree of similarity between them. We evaluate our method on human speech and musical instruments datasets. Experimental results show that, in comparison to other self-supervised and weakly supervised localization approaches, our model obtains significantly more complete segmentations, and learns a more effective measure of visual similarity.
